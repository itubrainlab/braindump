brAIn dump can now track the eyes with a webcam, through
[OpenGP3](https://github.com/itubrainlab/opengp3) 0.5.0's webcam eye tracking,
and it says so wherever the data goes.

## Webcam eye tracking

- **Chosen, never a fallback.** In the GP3 device's settings, **Tracker**
  chooses the Gazepoint GP3 or GP3 HD, or a webcam. With a Gazepoint tracker
  chosen and none plugged in, the device refuses to connect. It never records
  from a webcam instead.
- **Which webcam.** **Automatic** takes the camera OpenGP3 prefers, a built-in
  one first. You can also choose a camera from the list. It is remembered by
  the system's identifier, as the Video Stream remembers its camera.
- **Far less accurate.** Expect 2–5° instead of 0.5°. Tracking is sensitive to
  head movement and light, and there is no pupil size. The device page shows
  OpenGP3's warning in red while the webcam is connected, and so does the
  status bar's eye icon.
- **In the recording.** The sidecars give `ManufacturersModelName` `Webcam` and
  no manufacturer. The webcam's frame rate is the `SamplingFrequency`, and
  OpenGP3's warning is in `TrackerSetup`.
- **Calibrations.** A webcam's calibrations are saved apart from the GP3's
  (`webcam_…json`). Neither is loaded for the other tracker.
- The first use downloads MediaPipe's face model (about 4 MB) once. On macOS,
  the app needs camera access.

## Over the Open Gaze API

An `opengp3 server` 0.5.0 serving a webcam, or a simulated GP3, is recognised
from its `PRODUCT_ID`. The server's `WARNING` is shown on the device page and
written into `TrackerSetup`.

## Changes

- OpenGP3 0.5.0, with MediaPipe for its webcam eye tracking.
- CI: every push and pull request runs a quick check, Linux only and without
  the tests that record through simulated devices. Each release runs every
  test on Linux, Windows and macOS, then builds and checks the packages.
- A new logo: the brAIn lab brain, streaming binary.

## Install

The Windows zip, the macOS DMG (Apple Silicon, macOS 14+) or the Linux tarball
(x86_64, glibc 2.34+). The first launch downloads g.Pype 4.2, g.tec's drivers
(gtec-unicorn, gtec-gds), OpenGP3, MediaPipe, NDI and the other libraries from
PyPI.
