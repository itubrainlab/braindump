Two changes to what brAIn dump records: eye tracking is filed as BIDS
specifies, and the devices are synchronised to the EEG's own time.

## Eye tracking under `beh/`

Following BIDS's eye-tracking specification, the GP3's files are behavioural
data and always go under `beh/`, also when EEG is recorded with them. The
run's events are written there too, with the `StimulusPresentation` (screen
distance, origin, resolution and size) that gaze-on-screen data requires.
Calibration errors are given in pixels with `CalibrationUnit`, and
`EyeTrackerDistance` is added.

## Synchronisation

- **One clock.** Every device is time-stamped on one monotonic clock, so a
  change of the system time during a recording cannot shift one device
  against another.
- **The EEG's start, measured.** Every sample's acquisition time (corrected
  for Bluetooth bursts and the link's delay) is kept, and the first sample's
  time is fitted from all of them, rather than estimated from when the first
  sample reached the file (which could be some 30 ms off).
- **The EEG's real rate.** The headset's clock is never exactly 250 Hz; its
  rate is measured over each recording, and event onsets, EEG sample numbers
  and every `StartTime` are given in the EEG's own time through it, so they
  stay on the right sample however long the recording. `_eeg.json` reports
  the measurement under `RecordingTiming`.
- **Steadier clock mapping** for the Polar H10, the Shimmer and a remote GP3,
  and a **Bluetooth delay** setting for the Polar H10 and the Shimmer, as the
  Unicorn has.

A new test sends a UDP trigger (placed on the EEG by g.Pype) and an LSL
marker (placed by brAIn dump) at the same instant: they land on the same EEG
sample, within one sample (4 ms).

## Install

As for 0.1.0: the Windows zip, the macOS DMG (Apple Silicon, macOS 14+) or
the Linux tarball (x86_64, glibc 2.34+). The first launch downloads g.Pype,
g.tec's Unicorn driver and the other libraries from PyPI.

Recordings made with 0.1.0 keep their layout; new ones use the one above.
